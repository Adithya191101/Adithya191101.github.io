Project Overview

This project implements Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation of deformable objects on a low-cost SO-101 robot arm. Two architectures were explored: ACT (80% success) and SmolVLA (85% success via curriculum training). The project spans 17 technical challenges across two phases, culminating in a curriculum training pipeline with optimized inference parameters that achieved 85% success on 20+ continuous evaluation episodes.

Gripper Oscillation Diagnosis

All 9 SmolVLA models with vastly different hyperparameters showed identical behavior: the robot approaches correctly but gets jittery near the grasp point. Added action logging during inference to capture every prediction — revealing the root cause.

Finding: Gripper Oscillates Between Open and Closed

t=0.0s   gripper=0.48   (open)
t=5.6s   gripper=31.04 (closed)
t=11.8s gripper=2.29   (open!)
t=18.1s gripper=36.53 (closed)
t=31.3s gripper=-0.11 (open!)
t=37.7s gripper=32.78 (closed)

Max step-to-step gripper change: 30.1 degrees in a single step.

Root Cause: Training Data Imbalance

  • 76% of frames: Gripper open (approach phase) — normalizes to -0.54 sigma
  • 12% of frames: Gripper grasping — normalizes to +2.21 sigma
  • Model loss dominated by approach behavior; grasping requires a rare high-confidence prediction
  • RTC prefix guidance amplifies uncertainty between consecutive chunks

Phase 2: Curriculum Training to 85% Success

After diagnosing the gripper oscillation, we adopted a curriculum training approach [Bengio et al., 2009] inspired by iterative policy refinement in Diffusion Policy [Chi et al., 2023]. The key insight: rather than training from scratch, iteratively refine a pretrained policy through multiple stages, allowing the model to specialize for the target object's unique properties.

The brain toy presents specific manipulation challenges: it is deformable (changes shape under gripper pressure), has irregular geometry (no consistent grasp surface), and is elastic (bounces away on imprecise contact). These properties demand both precision and adaptability.

Methodology

Step 1 — LoRA Fine-Tuning: Applied Low-Rank Adaptation [Hu et al., 2021] targeting q_proj/v_proj in the language model expert and state/action projections to efficiently explore the action manifold.
Step 2 — Full Expert Training: Enabled full training of the action expert head while keeping the pretrained SmolVLM2-500M backbone frozen. This leverages pretrained visual features through Conditional Flow Matching [Lipman et al., 2023].
Step 3 — Curriculum Training: Three progressive iterations, each starting from the previous checkpoint.

Inference Optimization — The Critical Breakthrough

The single most impactful discovery was tuning the flow matching denoising process at inference time. Default num_steps=10 produced insufficient precision — the model oscillated between multiple grasping strategies. Increasing to num_steps=20 allowed the model to commit to a single strategy, while reducing n_action_steps from 20 to 10 enabled more frequent visual feedback during the critical grasping phase. This change alone improved success from ~50% to 85%.

Dataset Quality and Prompt Engineering

Comprehensive dataset auditing revealed a garbage episode (533 frames of static robot with zero action variance) corrupting the training signal. Removed this alongside exact prompt alignment between training data ("Grab the brain") and evaluation. Verified: 239 clean episodes, 99,845 frames, continuous indices.

Evaluation Protocol

All benchmarks were conducted with the policy running continuously without stopping or restarting between episodes. Restarting the policy resets the stochastic state of the flow matching denoising process, eliminating the natural action variability the model relies on. Continuous execution provides a more rigorous evaluation of real-world performance.

Hardware constraints: Results achieved with low-quality cameras (Logitech 720p front + SO-101 kit wrist camera) and only 2 of 3 supported camera inputs. The pretrained SigLIP vision encoder's robustness to image quality degradation enabled effective performance despite these limitations.

ACT vs SmolVLA: Architectural Comparison

Aspect ACT SmolVLA
Vision ResNet18 (end-to-end) Frozen SigLIP (pretrained)
Action prediction Deterministic Stochastic (flow matching)
Language None Language-conditioned
Camera robustness Brittle to displacement Handles displacement
Lighting robustness Degrades under shifts Consistent
Success rate 80% 85%

Grasping behavior: ACT executes deterministic trajectories — identical every time. SmolVLA's stochastic flow matching produces slight variation, enabling natural exploration when grasping deformable objects that may shift or bounce. Recovery: SmolVLA re-plans every 10 steps from fresh visual state; ACT repeats failed trajectories. Multi-object scaling: SmolVLA's language conditioning enables curriculum training across multiple objects without catastrophic forgetting — architecturally impossible with ACT.

Critical Debugging Discoveries

Seventeen challenges identified and systematically resolved across two phases of development (December 2025 — March 2026).

Phase 1 (7 challenges)

1. Language Instruction Mismatch
VLA models create different embeddings for different text. Exact training task string required. Fixed 0% to 33% success.
2. Camera Configuration Swap
Physical cameras reversed, causing complete spatial reasoning failure. Built preview tool to verify feeds.
3. Overfitting (15.87 epochs)
Optimized to 6.5 epochs — 2.5x faster training with better generalization.
4-7. Calibration, Camera Angle, Starting State, Config
Systematic resolution of hardware drift, visual consistency, and framework compatibility issues.

Phase 2 (10 challenges)

8. Training Methodology
Literature review revealed correct config (batch=64, lr=1e-4, frozen vision). The issue was methodology, not model capacity.
9. Normalization Mismatch (use_degrees)
LeRobot v0.5+ changed defaults. Dataset recorded with RANGE_M100_100 required explicit use_degrees=False.
10. Garbage Episode in Dataset
Episode 240: 533 static frames with zero action variance. Removed from local and HuggingFace Hub.
11. Task Text Mismatch (Refined)
Same bug class as #1, rediscovered with different text. Dataset: "Grab the brain" vs eval: full description.
12. Flow Matching Denoising Precision — THE Breakthrough
Default num_steps=10 caused mode-switching between grasping strategies. Doubling to 20 + halving n_action_steps to 10 achieved ~50% to 85%. Single most impactful discovery.
13. Batch Size / Linear Scaling
Batch=200 underperformed without LR scaling [Goyal et al., 2017]. Batch=64 with 70K steps gave best results.
14-17. Curriculum Training, Remote Inference, Recording, Disk
Progressive checkpoint refinement, FastAPI server on RunPod, shared frame buffer for recording, storage management.

Key Lessons Learned

Key Insights

  • Inference parameters can matter as much as training: Doubling denoising steps was the single largest improvement in the entire project
  • Curriculum training outperforms training from scratch: Progressive checkpoint refinement converges faster and to lower loss
  • Pretrained vision provides environmental robustness: Frozen SigLIP handles camera displacement and lighting variation without retraining
  • Language conditioning enables multi-object scaling: Different task prompts map to different action subspaces, preventing catastrophic forgetting
  • Data quality has outsized impact: One garbage episode (0.5% of data) measurably degraded grasping performance
  • Stochastic policies suit deformable objects: Flow matching's natural variation helps recover from failed grasps on elastic objects
